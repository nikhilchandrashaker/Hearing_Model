Set how loud, how many hours a day, and for how many years. Sensory hair cells in the cochlea are lost starting at the high-pitch end, and the audiogram shows the characteristic dip around 4 kHz. The animation is an illustrative model; the data panel is from a synthetic dataset. Not medical advice.

Dose follows the NIOSH convention: 85 dBA for 8 hours = 100% of a daily dose, and every +3 dB halves the safe time. Noise-years = daily dose × years. Injury f = 1 − e−noise-years/15. Loss at 4 kHz = 50f dB above a 5 dB baseline. Hair cells lost = 0.8f. These curves are my illustrative choices, not from a study. Age, genetics and medications also matter and aren't modeled here.
